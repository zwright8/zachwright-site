The exact review

Six checks between a listing and a defensible decision.

Every finding links to public source evidence at a stated cutoff. Missing evidence stays unknown instead of becoming a favorable assumption.

01

Funding and payout evidence

Separate an advertised amount from reserved funds, identify the payout rail and fees, and name the public evidence that supports each claim.

02

Claim window, eligibility, and competition

Check whether work is still available, who else is implementing it, and whether the buyer can satisfy the public eligibility rules.

03

Acceptance, access, and third-party dependencies

Identify the passing test, required hardware or accounts, and any maintainer, platform, or external adoption dependency.

04

Rights, compliance, and required approvals

Surface public contribution terms and any spend, contract, identity, tax, wallet, or other approval gate before work begins.

05

Bounded work estimate

State delivery hours, known fees, and paid-case return without inventing a collection probability or calling it expected value.

06

GO, HOLD, or NO-GO

Receive the recommendation, unresolved facts, and the smallest public verification step that could change the decision.

Before you request scope

Direct answers.

What may I put in a scope request?

Only public bounty URLs and public rules. Never include personal, private, credential, customer, wallet, or payment data.

Will WrightOps contact the payer or claim the work?

No. The review uses public evidence only and does not reserve, claim, message, implement, submit, or negotiate.

Does GO guarantee payment?

No. GO means the evidenced scope clears the stated review gates at the cutoff. Eligibility, acceptance, payout, profit, and every other outcome remain unguaranteed.

What happens when evidence is missing?

Missing evidence stays unknown. The report names the unresolved fact and the smallest public step that could verify it.

What happens if WrightOps cannot deliver?

The full purchase price is refunded for an undeliverable accepted report, duplicate charge, missing agreed delivery, or material scope mismatch. WrightOps absorbs any retained processor fee.
